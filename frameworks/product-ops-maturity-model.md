# Product Ops Maturity Model

Use this model to assess where your Product Operations capability stands today and to choose what to build next. It covers six dimensions across four levels.

---

## The Four Levels

| Level | Name | In one line |
|-------|------|-------------|
| 1 | Ad hoc | Processes depend on individuals. Each team works its own way. |
| 2 | Defined | Core processes are documented and used by most teams. |
| 3 | Managed | Processes are measured, and the data drives decisions. |
| 4 | Strategic | Product Operations shapes direction before execution begins. |

---

## The Model

| Dimension | 1: Ad hoc | 2: Defined | 3: Managed | 4: Strategic |
|-----------|-----------|------------|------------|--------------|
| **Planning** | Roadmaps built team by team on different timelines | One planning calendar and shared templates | Plans tied to OKRs, with capacity and dependencies checked | Planning informs strategy, and tradeoffs are made at portfolio level |
| **Intake and prioritization** | Requests arrive through side channels, and the loudest voice wins | One intake path and a documented scoring model | Scores are tracked, and decisions are logged and revisited | Intake data shows demand patterns that shape the roadmap |
| **Launch readiness** | Readiness is checked informally near launch day | A standard checklist with named owners | Readiness is reviewed at set gates, and launch outcomes are measured | Launch tiers are matched to business impact, and lessons feed the next launch |
| **Data and insights** | Metrics are pulled on request, and definitions differ by team | Shared metric definitions and a standard dashboard | Reviews run on the data, and leading indicators are tracked | Insights reach leaders before they ask and change decisions |
| **Cross-functional alignment** | Alignment happens in hallway conversations | A RACI and regular cross-functional reviews | Decision rights are clear, and escalations are rare and fast | Functions plan together, and conflicts surface during planning |
| **Tooling and AI** | Spreadsheets and manual status collection | Standard tools with consistent usage | Reporting is automated, and tools are integrated | AI supports reporting, risk detection, and intake, with human review |

---

## How to Assess

1. Gather three to five people from Product, Engineering, and a go-to-market function.
2. For each dimension, each person picks the level that describes most teams today. Score what happens in practice, not what is documented.
3. Compare scores. Where they differ by two or more levels, discuss why. The disagreement is often the most useful finding.
4. Record the agreed level and one piece of evidence for it.

|-----------|---------------|----------|--------------------------|

## How to Move Up

- **Raise the lowest dimension first.** A capability two levels behind the others limits the rest.
- **Move one level at a time.** Skipping from Ad hoc to Managed usually produces dashboards nobody trusts.
- **Pick two dimensions per half year.** More than that spreads the team too thin to finish any of them.
- **Tie each move to a business outcome.** For example, "move intake to Defined so that roadmap changes per quarter drop from [X] to [Y]."

| From | To | What it usually takes |
|------|----|-----------------------|
| 1 | 2 | Write the process down, name an owner, and train the teams |
| 2 | 3 | Add measures, review them on a cadence, and act on what they show |
| 3 | 4 | Bring Product Operations into strategy and planning discussions as a contributor |
